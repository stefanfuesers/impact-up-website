Coaching · Organisational Development · Impact Orientation

Stefan Füsers

I accompany small groups in finding their direction – and then in actually going there.

Who works here

Impact happens where a group comes to an understanding about its direction.

That is what twenty years in peace and development work have taught me – in Cambodia, in Kosovo, in Hamburg. The same moment kept returning: a team works hard and works well, and still it passes by the question that actually matters. Because nobody ever asked out loud what the whole thing is aiming at.

That is exactly where I begin. With the leadership I clarify where things are meant to go. With the team I work on finding the way there – in conversation, at the wall, in small steps. What emerges belongs to the group, not to me.

Under the roof of Impact Up I take this thinking further — here is where I come from and what I bring along.

Experience

What I bring along.

I founded Impact Up, the institute for systemic impact orientation, in 2026. Until 2025 I spent six years as an adviser for planning, monitoring and evaluation (PME) in the Civil Peace Service (CPS) of GIZ (Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit) in Cambodia. That is where I learned impact orientation in practice: with a CPS programme, with international experts and eight very diverse partner organisations. In peace and dealing-with-the-past work, project impact was always part of a programme impact of the CPS and at the same time part of the impact of the partner organisations – an international tribunal, psychological service providers, memorial sites, youth movements, universities. Capacity development was often a core element, and project impact not seldom organisational development as well.

The CPS programme was connected with the other CPS programmes in Cambodia, so that impact on the country as a whole was equally intended; added to that were many further PME advisers of the CPS in other countries, with whom we regularly reflected on methods, structure and ways of working. In this way I brought numerous actors in the field in Cambodia into resonance with one another and strengthened a network of people active for peaceful coexistence. In planning and reflection workshops, methods from peace work itself came into play, from conflict transformation for instance, along with my experience as a yoga teacher and from Nonviolent Communication.

Since returning to Germany I have been working intensively on theories and methods for putting participation and co-creation into practice, on systemic approaches and a systemic stance. Numerous authors give me a space to reflect on the wealth of material from my time in Cambodia. Since December 2025 I have been deepening this in the training as a systemic organisational developer at WISPO. Literature on Systems Change and on co-creation has created a lot of resonance. With Impact Up I now take the methods for planning, monitoring and reflecting together with the methods of peace work – dialogue, conflict transformation, dealing with the past and with trauma – into systemic practice, and develop approaches for systemic impact orientation aimed at changing society. Context-specific, with the empathy and the sense it takes to truly hear, see and feel what is at stake.

Thematically I bring a broad background: before the peace work in Kosovo and Cambodia, economics and international studies at university. I have been shaped by many years of change work with social-ecological movements, above all for a basic income and degrowth. For the Climate Service Center in Hamburg I examined how the participation of practice actors in scientific processes can be adequately taken into account in evaluations. And in nearly every role I have initiated and organised events – from small festivals through extensive trainings to large conferences.

The past year belonged to artificial intelligence: since returning to Germany I have used it to build up systemic organisational development and Impact Up — and along with it a cooperation network reaching from IP-GO through the Hamburg self-employed network and the EH returnees to KURVE Wustrow. What is growing out of that is over on About Us.

What hangs on the wall is what the group thought. My work is getting it there.

Artificial Intelligence

These systems are binding themselves to the world right now.

Artificial means made. The word comes from art, and it says something precise about these systems: they are made from what people have thought, written and ordered. What comes towards us there is a condensation of human knowledge – and as with everything made, the execution decides what it is worth.

So what interests me is less what these systems can do. What interests me is what they bind themselves to. In an organisation that means quite concretely: to its language, its impact logic, its decision paths, its standards for what counts as good. This happens anyway, as soon as someone starts working. Whether it turns out well can be shaped.

Honestly, the most fascinating part is the lack of understanding: what goes on inside these systems is not fully understood by anyone — not even by those who build them. I follow who is driving the development, which conceptual constructs are emerging there, and what the people close to the technology have already grasped. And the question of how to connect work like mine to such a black box strikes me as one of the most interesting there is at the moment.

What meets here are very different orders: the machine logic of these systems, the material and ecological contexts we are embedded in, the social systems in which people build identity and relationship, and the psychological states out of which they do so. How that hangs together, I have written out in an impulse.

I contribute most where these systems play a role in change processes. Whoever is building impact orientation or setting up a monitoring system can decide right away which parts of it nobody has to do by hand. How Impact Up works on this is under Integrating Artificial Intelligence.

What I think along the way gets connected, checked and given form – until a text comes out of it that can go out into the world.
Representation
At the end of the chain stands how I appear in the world: website, letters, documents. No image of me, but carried by what I hold to be right.

And what stays with people in all this

Years ago, Peter Kruse described what a highly networked system needs so that many contributions turn into something clever instead of just a lot of noise: processes that can evaluate qualitatively. That sorting work is available by machine today, and that is the real news. What cannot be handed over is the culture in which people bring their contributions in at all – the shared ground on which not everything has to be negotiated again, so that energy remains for what actually matters.

This culture grows in conversations, and it grows slowly. Over six years in the Civil Peace Service in Cambodia that was my biggest contribution, even if it looks like nothing in any report: being the one who was there long enough, who stayed in conversation with all sides and led the disagreements in such a way that a programme with very different partners grew a shared working culture – state bodies, civil society organisations, university, psychologists, international colleagues. That does not happen on the side, and it does not happen without resonance between people.

That is exactly why the division of labour is clear to me. The sorting work may go to the machine. Judgement, responsibility and the moment in the room stay with people. What is freed up is time for what only arises there.

Impact Orientation · Systemic Organisational Development · Coaching

Stefan Füsers

Founder of Impact Up. More than 15 years of experience in shaping and accompanying projects in peace work and in numerous social movements and organisations — Cambodia, Kosovo and Hamburg.

Stefan founded Impact Up, the institute for systemic impact orientation, in 2026. Until 2025 he spent six years as an adviser for planning, monitoring and evaluation (PME) in the Civil Peace Service (CPS) of GIZ (Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit) in Cambodia. There he gathered practical experience in impact orientation with a CPS programme, with international experts and eight very diverse partner organisations. In peace and dealing-with-the-past work, project impact was always part of a programme impact of the CPS and at the same time part of the impact of the partner organisations – an international tribunal, psychological service providers, memorial sites, youth movements, universities. Capacity development was often a core element, and project impact not seldom organisational development as well. Here he learned how complexity in systems can be met with appreciation – and how that keeps options for action open.

The whole CPS programme was connected with the other CPS programmes in Cambodia, so that impact on the country as a whole was equally intended; and not least there were many further PME advisers in the CPS of GIZ in other countries, who regularly reflected on methods, structure and ways of working. In this way Stefan brought numerous actors in the field in Cambodia into resonance with one another and strengthened a network of people active for peaceful coexistence. Methods from peace work itself, from conflict transformation for instance, came into such planning and reflection workshops again and again, as did his experience as a yoga teacher and from Nonviolent Communication.

Since returning to Germany, Stefan has been working intensively on theories and methods for how participation and co-creation can genuinely be realised, on systemic approaches and a systemic stance. Numerous authors offer a space to reflect on the material he brought back from his time in Cambodia. Since December 2025 he has been deepening this in the training as a systemic organisational developer at WISPO. Literature on Systems Change and on co-creation has created a lot of resonance. With Impact Up he now takes the methods for planning, monitoring and reflecting together with the methods of peace work – dialogue, conflict transformation, dealing with the past and with trauma – into systemic practice, and develops approaches for systemic impact orientation aimed at changing society. Context-specific, with the empathy and the sense of what it takes to truly hear, see and feel what is at stake.

Thematically, Stefan brings a broad background from his time before the peace work in Kosovo and Cambodia – from economics and international studies at university. Many years of change work with social-ecological movements, above all for a basic income and degrowth, have shaped him as well. For the Climate Service Center in Hamburg he examined how the participation of practice actors in scientific processes can be adequately taken into account in evaluations. And in nearly every role he has initiated and organised events again and again – from small festivals through extensive trainings to large conferences.

Systemic Organisational Development

The training in systemic organisational development at WISPO is exciting for Stefan above all because systemic methods make it possible to offer support more broadly. The methods and the stance make it possible to genuinely support people even without deep contextual knowledge. Together with his consulting experience, Stefan now wants to put this knowledge to work for good ideas and projects – and go on making a meaningful difference in the world. The active network-building of these years, out of which much of Impact Up is emerging today (more on that under About Us), opens the way to it. As services of the institute, this begins with Project Coaching and Impact Consulting.

What hangs on the wall is what the group thought. Stefan's work is getting it there.

Artificial Intelligence

The developments in artificial intelligence are fascinating and remarkably dynamic. Building Impact Up turned into many hours of iterative collaboration with AI. Within his self-employment, Stefan began testing and trying out the possibilities playfully, again and again; by now he uses many of them productively as well, to bring things into the world. Tools like the Session Builder — and perhaps bringing Impact Up itself into the world on his own within a few months — would have been unthinkable without support from AI. The experience is manifold, and briefly summed up: AI can by now do nearly everything that can be done on a computer. The difficulty lies in connecting the sense of purpose to the AI properly before the work, and in connecting the check on that sense back the other way afterwards.

What interests Stefan most today is what AI systems bind themselves to: the language, the impact logic and the standards of a piece of work. What exactly happens inside them is not known even to the developers — which makes practice in working with AI a valuable resource. To that end Stefan keeps following the latest developments and the perspectives of people who work with them even more productively. More and more questions of ethics and responsibility arise in this context — and which things we deliberately want to share only with people. How you want AI in your processes, and how that can work, is something Stefan is glad to accompany: Integrating Artificial Intelligence.
